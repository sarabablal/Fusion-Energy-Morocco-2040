# Is Fusion Energy a Realistic Option for Morocco's Energy Mix by 2040?

Independent research paper examining the physical, technical, and economic
feasibility of fusion energy for Morocco, compared directly against the
country's existing renewable, fossil fuel, and nuclear cost benchmarks —
including the Noor Ouarzazate solar complex.

**Author:** Sara Bablal — Ouarzazate, Morocco

---

## Abstract

Morocco imports 94% of its primary energy and remains heavily dependent on
fossil fuels, creating both economic vulnerability and conflict with its
climate commitments. While Morocco has set ambitious renewable energy
targets, solar and wind remain intermittent, raising the question of
whether fusion energy could offer a viable complement by 2040. This
research examines the physical, technical, and economic feasibility of
fusion energy for Morocco's energy mix, drawing on current fusion research
timelines, cost projections, and comparisons with Morocco's existing
energy infrastructure. The physics and engineering challenges of
deuterium-tritium fusion are reviewed, along with the current state of
major fusion programs including ITER and private initiatives such as
Commonwealth Fusion Systems. A hypothetical 500 MW fusion plant is used to
assess Morocco's grid capacity, water availability, land requirements, and
financial feasibility, with direct comparisons to Morocco's existing
solar, wind, and gas infrastructure, including the Noor Ouarzazate solar
complex. The results show that fusion remains substantially more
expensive than Morocco's current energy sources on a capital cost basis,
and that no major fusion facility is expected to reach commercial
operation before the late 2030s at the earliest. While fusion could
theoretically provide dispatchable power that intermittent renewables
cannot, Morocco's fiscal constraints, limited technical infrastructure,
and existing renewable energy trajectory make large-scale fusion
deployment unlikely by 2040. The research concludes that Morocco's most
realistic path forward involves early-stage engagement, such as research
partnerships and workforce development, rather than direct investment in
fusion power generation.

**Keywords:** fusion energy, Morocco, renewable energy, energy security,
levelized cost of energy, energy transition, 2040

## Structure

1. **Introduction** — Morocco's current energy situation and the case for exploring fusion
2. **Fusion Physics and Current Status** — how D-T fusion works, confinement approaches, and global progress (ITER, NIF, private companies)
3. **Morocco's Grid and Fusion Cost Outlook** — grid capacity, land and water requirements, and Morocco's technical workforce
4. **Comparing the Economics of Fusion in Morocco** — capital costs, capacity factors, and financing models
5. **Fusion vs. Solar** — a direct comparison with Morocco's Noor Ouarzazate solar complex
6. **Barriers and Risks** — dependency and strategic uncertainty
7. **Conclusion** — final assessment and recommended next steps for Morocco
